## (b) EUDR commodity scope (for §3.2 cross-sector extension)
- Regulation (EU) 2023/1115 covers **seven commodities: cattle, cocoa, coffee, oil palm, rubber, soya and wood**, plus certain derived products listed in **Annex I**. Products must not come from land **deforested after 31 December 2020**. In force since 29 June 2023; applies to **large and medium operators from 30 Dec 2026**, micro/small from **30 Jun 2027**, and products added by Delegated Act from **30 Dec 2027** [EC-EUDR].
- The May 2026 simplification package proposed adding certain palm-oil derivatives and soluble instant coffee to Annex I [HL-EUDR2026].
- **Relevance:** Malaysia exports two other EUDR commodities besides palm oil, **rubber** and **cocoa** (and timber). SawitSense's satellite-geolocation and explain-to-audit evidence log transfer directly to them.

## (c) Rubber traceability challenge in Malaysia
- About **90 % of rubber cultivation is by smallholders on scattered plots**. Of **508,850 rubber smallholders** (2023 RISDA census), **311,878 hold a digital PAT-G permit** recording land ownership, **geolocation** and tree profile [EDGE-RUBBER2026].
- Malaysia's response is the **MSNR (Malaysian Sustainable Natural Rubber)** standard under the Rubber Act, with **MSNR Trace**, a mandatory digital transaction declaration system. Non-compliance carries fines of up to RM50,000 and/or 2 years' jail [EDGE-RUBBER2026].
- Gaps: full plantation-to-exporter traceability is hard with scattered plots. MSNR covers only domestic rubber, so only ~10 % of glove latex can meet it. Natural-rubber output fell 12.9 % to 336,676 t in 2025 [EDGE-RUBBER2026].
- *Extension argument for §3.2:* rubber faces the same smallholder geolocation and traceability problem as palm oil. SawitSense's satellite plot verification, mobile capture and evidence log could feed MSNR Trace, and Ganoderma-style disease screening could extend to rubber leaf diseases (disease claim not researched; keep it as a proposal).
